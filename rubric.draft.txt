======================================================================
PRODUCT MANAGER (PM)
======================================================================

Criterion name: Operational proximity
What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally done operational work in a
workflow-heavy business (logistics, freight, supply chain, field ops, or similar):
handled shipments, documents, exceptions or customer escalations themselves, not only
through software, reports or APIs. Strong (8-10): 2+ years hands-on in the operation,
with specifics (volumes, document types, counterparties). Weak (0-3): only built or sold
software for such businesses from a distance, or no operational exposure at all.
Weight: 30%

Criterion name: Self-started fixes others adopted
What a strong candidate looks like: Spotted a problem no one had assigned them, built a
working fix with the tools available (a spreadsheet, a prototype, a new process), and
the CV says other people started using it (team adoption, other teams, became standard).
Strong: at least one clear example with who adopted it and how fast. Weak: only
assigned features or responsibilities described, or a fix with no sign anyone used it.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Candour about failure
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV describes something that did not work and
what they did about it: a post-mortem, killing their own feature on data, a lost deal
analysed, an incident owned to closure. Strong: a specific failure plus the concrete
change that followed. Weak: every bullet is a success metric.
Weight: 15%

Criterion name: Ownership under pressure
What a strong candidate looks like: Resolved a time-critical problem themselves, with
limited support, without escalating: an overnight customs hold, a vendor change with no
notice, a spike in volume with no extra headcount. Strong: a named situation, what they
did, and the outcome. Weak: generic "worked in a fast-paced environment" language.
Weight: 15%

Criterion name: Translating between operators and builders
What a strong candidate looks like: Has turned what frontline users actually do into
something engineers could build, and caught mismatches between the software and the
real workflow (discovery with operators, field requirements into specs, coordinating a
fix between users and engineering). Strong: a specific mismatch they uncovered and the
change it led to. Weak: lists discovery, PRDs or user interviews as activities with no
finding.
Weight: 15%

======================================================================
SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER (SPM)
Same criteria, higher bar, especially on how independently they operated.
======================================================================

Criterion name: Operational proximity
What a strong candidate looks like: As for PM, and they have also used that operational
knowledge to shape a product or process that serves many operators (e.g. rolled a tool
out to multiple client teams). Strong: 3+ years combined in operations and in building
for operations. Weak: operational exposure only as an observer or through a vendor.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Self-started fixes others adopted
What a strong candidate looks like: Repeatedly (2+ examples) created something unasked
that spread beyond their own team: adopted across teams, by clients, or became a core
product feature, with them as the sole or lead owner. Strong: sole owner of a product
area or a 0-to-1 build that others now depend on. Weak: a single team-level example, or
ownership shared with a senior lead.
Weight: 30%

Criterion name: Candour about failure
What a strong candidate looks like: As for PM, and they made the call to stop, reverse
or redirect work (killed features, changed strategy, walked away from a deal) and
explained why to others. Strong: a decision that cost them something and the reasoning
shared with the team or customers. Weak: failures mentioned only as problems solved.
Weight: 15%

Criterion name: Ownership under pressure
What a strong candidate looks like: Owned high-stakes outcomes with no one above them to
escalate to: incidents, migrations, key accounts or launches where they were the final
decision-maker. Strong: a named, high-stakes situation where they were accountable end
to end. Weak: pressure handled as part of a larger team, or under a manager's direction.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Translating between operators and builders
What a strong candidate looks like: Has set direction for engineers from operator
insight: decided what to build and what not to, with the engineering team trusting
their calls. Strong: evidence engineers followed their product decisions (e.g. direct
feedback, calls that changed a roadmap). Weak: coordinated between teams but did not
make the decisions.
Weight: 10%
