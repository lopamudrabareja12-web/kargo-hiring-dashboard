KARGO HIRING RUBRIC: PRODUCT MANAGER AND SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER
Built from the patterns in 8 past hire profiles, not from the job descriptions.

=====================================================================
HOW TO SCORE
=====================================================================

Score each criterion 0, 1 or 2, using only what is written in the CV.

  2 = Strong. The CV meets every part of the "Strong" test.
  1 = Partial. The CV meets the "Partial" test.
  0 = Weak or absent. The CV meets the "Weak" test, or there is nothing
      relevant.

Weighted score (out of 100) = sum of (score x weight) / 2

Rules that apply to every criterion:
  - Score only what is written. Don't give credit for what the
    candidate "probably" did.
  - Don't count the same thing twice:
      * Other people using something the candidate built (a tool,
        tracker, template or document) is scored under "The fix spread".
      * Other people changing a step, check or decision because of a
        lesson from a failure is scored under "Post-mortem others now
        follow".
      * A single CV line can't score in both criteria.
  - User-side results are counted changes in what the customer or user
    has to deal with. These are:
      * support tickets or queries
      * delays or on-time departures
      * penalties or inspection outcomes
      * onboarding, integration or time-to-value
      * errors or exceptions the user faced
  - Company-side results do not count as user-side. These are:
      * revenue, ARR, pipeline, quota or CAC
      * churn
      * adoption %
      * number of features shipped
      * system speed, uptime or processing lag


=====================================================================
PRODUCT MANAGER
=====================================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Criterion name: Fixed what they saw
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The CV has all three of these:
  (a) The trigger is stated. It names a problem that colleagues or
      customers were having in their work, using wording like "found
      that...", "when X changed...", "noticed...".
  (b) The candidate built the fix themselves: a tool, tracker,
      prototype, process or document.
  (c) Nothing says the work was assigned. There is no "tasked with",
      "responsible for" or "asked to".
  Examples:
    - Rohan: built an Excel tracker "after finding that the team had
      no reliable way to know where each shipment stood."
    - Sunita: "When the company's FMS vendor changed their data export
      format without notice, independently redesigned the team's
      documentation intake and filing workflow over a weekend."
Partial (1):
  The CV has one of these:
  - Unassigned work with no stated trigger. Examples: Vikram's "Built
    and socialised a PRD template"; Rahul's "Launched Ventus's first
    customer case study programme".
  - A stated trigger, but someone else built the fix. Example: Meghna
    found a product limitation and wrote the bug report; engineering
    fixed it.
  - A trigger stated only as a system metric. Example: Preetham's
    "after identifying query degradation at scale".
Weak (0):
  The CV has only assigned work ("led the migration of...", "owns the
  X module") or general claims ("proactive", "self-starter").
Weight: 25%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Criterion name: The fix spread
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  Other people used something the candidate built. The CV names both:
  - who used it: a specific group, such as a "12-member team", "2
    other regional teams" or the "full CS team"
  - a number, a timeframe or a sign that it lasted, such as "within
    two weeks", "now uses" or "retained permanently"
  Examples:
    - Rohan: "adopted across the 12-member operations team within two
      weeks."
    - Lavanya: dashboard "adopted by 2 other regional teams."
Partial (1):
  Adoption is claimed, but either the group or the number/time is
  missing. Examples: "rolled out to the team"; "became widely used".
Weak (0):
  Words like "socialised", "presented", "proposed" or "shared" with no
  sign that anyone used it. Or the candidate built something only they
  used. Example: Preetham's monitoring dashboard.
Weight: 20%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Criterion name: In the room when it broke
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The CV has all three of these:
  (a) a specific live failure, such as a hold, outage, inspection,
      escalation or vendor failure
  (b) the candidate personally dealt with the outside party, such as
      a customer, customs official, carrier, vendor or authority
  (c) the outcome is stated
  Examples:
    - Meghna: "coordinated directly with the CHA and customs officer
      through the night to resolve before the client became aware —
      shipment departed on schedule."
    - Sunita: "Liaised with Customs Preventive and Audit teams during
      two shipment-level inspections; both closed without penalties."
Partial (1):
  The CV has one of these:
  - A live failure resolved only inside the company, with no outside
    party. Examples: Preetham's P1 on-call rotation; Rohan's
    PostgreSQL fix.
  - An outside party was involved, but the candidate's own contact
    with them, or the outcome, isn't stated.
Weak (0):
  No failure is described, or only general lines like "handled
  escalations" or "strong under pressure" with no specific event. Most
  CVs that list only successes score 0 here (Vikram and Rahul do).
Weight: 20%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Criterion name: Post-mortem others now follow
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The CV names a specific failure the candidate was part of, such as a
  lost deal, outage, missed deadline or failed feature, and shows all
  three of these:
  (a) The cause is written down in concrete terms, not just "what went
      wrong".
  (b) Someone else received it: a team, a customer or a manager.
  (c) The lesson changed how other people work. The CV names a step,
      check or decision that others now handle differently because of
      it. Other people using the write-up itself as a document doesn't
      count here; that belongs under "The fix spread".
  Example:
    - Aditya: "ran a post-mortem on the loss, documented root cause
      (misread of IT procurement cycle and wrong economic buyer
      identified), shared with the team; now standard practice before
      account qualification." The team now checks the procurement
      cycle and the economic buyer before qualifying an account.
Partial (1):
  A specific failure plus one or two of (a)-(c), but not all three.
  Examples:
    - Lavanya: wrote and shared the outage post-mortem, but the CV
      doesn't state the cause or any change in how others work.
    - Preetham: names the bug's cause and fixed it, but there is no
      write-up and no change in how others work.
  A line like "ran retrospectives every sprint" (a routine meeting,
  not a lesson from a specific failure) also scores 1.
Weak (0):
  - The CV lists only successes.
  - General lines like "learned from setbacks" or "comfortable with
    failure".
  - "Killed a feature" with no stated cause and no one told.
Weight: 10%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Criterion name: Measured in the user's pain
---------------------------------------------------------------------
How to count:
  List every quantified result in the candidate's two most recent
  roles. Mark each one as user-side or company-side, using the lists
  under HOW TO SCORE.
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  At least two user-side results, and user-side results make up at
  least half of all quantified results.
  Examples:
    - Lavanya: support tickets -60%, integration time -2 weeks,
      delivery exceptions -28%, first shipment on time.
    - Meghna: onboarding queries -35%, time-to-first-value -3 weeks,
      zero delays on temperature-sensitive cargo.
Partial (1):
  At least one user-side result, but they make up less than half of
  the results.
  Examples:
    - Vikram: support volume -22% and time-to-first-value -3 weeks,
      against features shipped, ARR and adoption %.
    - Rohan: support queries -35%, against processing lag and P1
      counts.
Weak (0):
  No user-side results at all.
  Examples:
    - Rahul: pipeline, CAC, MQL conversion.
    - Preetham: query time, uptime, processing lag.
Weight: 25%

PM weights: 25 + 20 + 20 + 10 + 25 = 100%


=====================================================================
SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER
=====================================================================
Same five criteria. To score 2 for SPM, the CV must pass the PM "Strong"
test AND the extra SPM bar. A CV that meets the PM "Strong" test but not
the SPM bar scores 1.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Criterion name: Fixed what they saw
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The PM "Strong" test, plus both of these:
  - The problem was outside the candidate's assigned area, or the fix
    needed a decision normally made above them.
  - The CV shows they made that call alone, with wording like
    "independently", "over a weekend", "without a product layer", and
    no "proposed to" or "got approval".
  Examples:
    - Rohan: as an Operations Executive, built the team's tracking
      system that nobody had assigned.
    - Sunita: "independently redesigned the team's documentation intake
      and filing workflow over a weekend."
Partial (1):
  The PM "Strong" test, but the fix stayed inside the candidate's own
  assigned area, or the CV mentions getting sign-off first.
Weak (0):
  The PM "Partial" or "Weak" test.
Weight: 30%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Criterion name: The fix spread
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The PM "Strong" test, plus one of these:
  - It was adopted outside the candidate's own team. Example: Lavanya,
    "adopted by 2 other regional teams".
  - It became a permanent part of how the company runs. Examples:
    Rohan, "now a core platform feature"; Sunita, "retained
    permanently by the team".
Partial (1):
  Adoption inside the candidate's own team only, with a group and a
  number or time named.
Weak (0):
  The PM "Partial" or "Weak" test.
Weight: 20%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Criterion name: In the room when it broke
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The PM "Strong" test, plus the CV states that nobody sat above or
  between the candidate and the outside party.
  Examples:
    - Meghna: "no escalation to management in the last 14 months."
    - Aditya: "no account manager layer between the client and
      execution."
Partial (1):
  The PM "Strong" test, but someone else led the resolution, or the
  candidate escalated it.
Weak (0):
  The PM "Partial" or "Weak" test.
Weight: 20%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Criterion name: Post-mortem others now follow
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The PM "Strong" test, plus both of these:
  - The candidate wrote the post-mortem themselves, not just
    contributed to it.
  - The lesson changed how people outside the candidate's own team
    work: another function, customers or partners. The CV names the
    step, check or decision those people now handle differently.
  Illustrative CV line (none of the 8 hires reaches this level):
    "Wrote the post-mortem on a failed carrier integration; sales now
    confirms the customer's EDI format before contract signature."
Partial (1):
  The PM "Strong" test, but the change stayed inside the candidate's
  own team (Aditya scores 1 at SPM level), or the candidate only
  contributed to the write-up.
Weak (0):
  The PM "Partial" or "Weak" test.
Weight: 15%

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Criterion name: Measured in the user's pain
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like (2):
  The PM "Strong" test, plus at least one user-side result held over a
  stated period of 6 months or more.
  Example:
    - Meghna: "zero delays on temperature-sensitive cargo across 8
      months handling this account."
Partial (1):
  The PM "Strong" test, but no user-side result has a stated period of
  6 months or more.
Weak (0):
  The PM "Partial" or "Weak" test.
Weight: 15%

SPM weights: 30 + 20 + 20 + 15 + 15 = 100%
